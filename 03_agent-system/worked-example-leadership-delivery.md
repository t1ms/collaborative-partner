# Delivered Result — "leadership material" example

This is the artifact the user receives at S6 (Close). It is NOT advice. It is their own thinking, debugged and made visible. Every claim traces back to a question they answered.

---

## 1. The Bedrock (load-bearing assumption, made explicit)
> The problem was resting on: *"One person's single comment = a fixed verdict on my leadership, and that verdict settles what I'm allowed to do."*

Stripped to zero, the only load-bearing fact is: **one piece of feedback** ("lacked strategic ownership") on one project.

## 2. Your Well-Formed Outcome (what you now own)
- **Positive & specific:** "Submit a promotion application showing two concrete examples of strategic ownership, and request a 15-minute meeting with my manager to discuss it."
- **Self-initiated:** entirely within your control — no one's permission required to draft it.
- **Sensory / evidence:** two named examples + a scheduled conversation = you'll know it landed.
- **Chunked:** draft examples this week → book the meeting next → submit.

## 3. Perspectives you considered
- **Their chair (2nd position):** if they saw two concrete examples of strategic ownership, the "lacked" label weakens on its own evidence.
- **Systemic:** if nobody applied unless already 100% seen as ready, the promotion pipeline would die — applying *is* how readiness gets tested.

## 4. Ecology check (trade-off, surfaced not hidden)
- If you apply and don't get it: you spend ~1 hour of prep + a little ego; you gain a concrete signal on what "strategic ownership" means to them and a real relationship conversation.
- Net: low-cost, high-information. The cost of *not* applying was the unexamined verdict standing unchallenged.

## 5. The Trace (audit — every question → pattern it surfaced)
| Turn | Pattern surfaced | Question asked |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | mind_reading | "What did you actually see or hear that told you they don't think you're leadership material?" |
| 3 | cause_effect | "How does their one comment actually mean you shouldn't apply — and what if it didn't?" |
| 4 | unspecified_referent | "When you say 'they' — was it the skip-level, or a wider group? What made it feel like one mind deciding?" |
| 5 | lost_performative | "Who says you shouldn't apply — where did that rule come from?" |
| 6 | outcome | "What do you want instead of 'not applying'? State it as something you control." |
| 7 | viewpoint | "If they saw two examples of strategic ownership, what happens to the 'lacked' label?" |
| 8 | ecology | "If you apply and don't get it — what's the actual trade-off?" |

**You leave with:** a decision you own, the assumption it rested on made visible, and a record you can reopen. Nothing was decided *for* you.
