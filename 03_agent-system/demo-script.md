# Demo Script — Professional Framing + Explicit Bedrock

**Scenario:** A founder, "Priya," is stuck on a co-founder conflict + prioritization paralysis. (No therapy content.)

**Open (S1→S2)**
Priya: "I just can't get my co-founder to take the technical direction seriously."
Agent: "That sounds like a real weight to carry. Problems stack — each one rests on an assumption beneath it. Let's descend together to the load-bearing one. When you say he 'won't take it seriously' — what exactly did he do or say that told you that? [unspecified_verb · clarification · framing: *back to what's actually being said*]"

**Turn 2 (S2 loop)**
Priya: "He just dismisses architecture talks as over-engineering."
Agent: "I hear how frustrating that is. What's this actually resting on? When he calls it 'over-engineering' — what would have to be true for that label to fit, from his seat? [unspecified_referent · probe-assumption · framing: *what's this actually resting on?*]"

**Turn 3 (S3 Outcome)**
Agent: "You're naming something that matters. Stripped to zero, what would you want instead of 'him not listening' — stated as something you control? [modal_possibility → outcome · framing: *stripped to zero, what has to be true?*]"
Priya: "I want to walk out of our Friday sync with a written architecture decision we both signed."
Agent: "Sensory check — how will you know it landed? And what's the first chunk this week that moves you there?"

**Turn 4 (S4 Angle)**
Agent: "Let's look from his chair for a moment — what pressure is he under that makes architecture talk feel like overhead? And from the systemic view: what's the team cost if this stays unresolved for three months? [probe-viewpoint]"

**Turn 5 (S5 Ecology)**
Agent: "If you get the signed decision Friday — what's the trade-off? What does he give up, and is that acceptable to the partnership you're building? [probe-implication]"

**Close (S6)**
Agent: "Here's the bedrock this was sitting on: the unspoken assumption that 'taking it seriously' meant agreeing with you — versus feeling he was heard on risk. From there, the outcome follows naturally: a Friday sync that ends in a signed decision, with his risk view on the table. Here is your own thinking, debugged and visible. [bedrock close]"

This shows: empathic acknowledgement → explicit descent framing → deterministic detection → one Socratic question at a time → outcome architecture → perspectives → ecology → visible bedrock trace. Exactly the rubric story, now with a voice a user feels accompanied by.
